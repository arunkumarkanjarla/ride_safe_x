Compliance & government reporting

How incidents turn into official reports, which rules apply by region and how long each kind of data is kept.

Pipeline

From signal to report

Regions

Regulations by region

RegionRules to plan forWhat it means for the platform
IndiaMotor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines (MoRTH) and state rules; Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; CERT-In cyber incident directionsDriver verification and training records, panic button and tracking, fare caps where set by states, consent and data rights, incident reporting to CERT-In within hours.
European UnionGDPR; national taxi and private-hire lawsLawful basis per data use, data export and erasure on request, data kept in the EU region.
United StatesState and city TNC rules; CCPA/CPRA and other state privacy lawsInsurance proof, background checks per state, privacy notices and opt-outs.
Middle EastLocal transport authority rules (for example Dubai RTA); local data lawsLicensed operators and vehicles, data residency where required.

This is a planning list, not legal advice. Confirm current rules with a lawyer in each country before launch; they change often.

Retention

How long data is kept

DataKeep forThen
Trip records and invoicesAs required by tax law (often 7–8 years)Archive, then delete
GPS trails180 days (longer if linked to an incident)Delete
Call recordings90 days unless under investigationDelete
KYC documentsWhile active + period required by local rulesDelete
Audit logsAt least 3 yearsArchive
Incident evidenceUntil the case closes + legal hold periodDelete after legal sign-off

Retention periods are configuration per country, enforced by a nightly job that also writes an audit entry.
